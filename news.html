29 October 2026 · Google

Google plans to put ads in its AI Mode search answers in the UK by the end of 2026

Ask Google’s AI Mode for the best kettle under £50 and, before long, part of the answer could be an advert. In a blog post on 28 October, Google said it plans to bring ads in AI Mode to more countries in the last three months of 2026, naming the UK, Brazil and Mexico.

AI Mode is the tab in Google Search where you type a full question and get a written answer back. It reached the UK in July 2025, on the search results page and in the Google app for Android and iPhone.

Ten other countries get the ads now, among them Australia, Canada, India and New Zealand. Google says the ads will carry a “Sponsored” label. In the US it has also started showing ads on follow-up questions, so one can appear partway through a conversation. Google hasn’t said whether that version will come to the UK.

The post gives no exact UK date and says nothing about a way to turn the ads off.

What you can do

  • When AI Mode suggests a product or a shop, look for the “Sponsored” label before you click.
  • Check the price on one or two other sites before you buy something an AI answer recommends.

28 October 2026 · Bank of England and FCA

86% of UK finance firms now use AI, says Bank of England and FCA survey

Your bank, insurer or lender probably uses AI. On 26 October the Bank of England and the Financial Conduct Authority published their survey of AI in UK financial services, and 86% of the firms that replied said they use it. In 2024 the figure was 75%.

The survey got 196 responses: 101 larger firms and 95 smaller ones, spread across UK banks, international banks, insurers, lenders, investment firms and payment companies. Smaller firms are catching up, with 73% using AI. Most of those mainly use the same kind of tool as ChatGPT, known as a large language model.

AI agents, which carry out tasks rather than just answer questions, have spread too. 80% of firms said they have agents deployed, and another 14% plan to within a year. Only 2% said they let agents act with broad autonomy.

So AI may already sit behind some of the letters, chats and decisions you get from financial firms. The rules on complaints stay the same, whoever or whatever made the mistake.

What you can do

  • If a bank, insurer or lender gets something wrong, complain to the firm first and explain how you want it fixed.
  • If the firm doesn’t reply within 8 weeks, or within 15 days for a fraud, scam or payment complaint, or you’re unhappy with its final answer, take it to the Financial Ombudsman Service for free.

27 October 2026 · Google

Google’s AI game for 11 to 14 year olds adds a brain-mapping quest

On 26 October Google Research and the Stanford Accelerator for Learning launched Polar Peaks, the third quest in their AI Quests series for students aged 11 to 14. Players take the role of Google researchers, map neurons in the brain and help a game character who has lost their memory.

Along the way they clean data and test an AI model, with mentors in the game guiding them. The point is to show that AI needs people checking its work. The quest ends with students hearing from two Google researchers, including one behind the recent fruit fly brain map. The first two quests covered flood forecasting and screening for an eye disease linked to diabetes.

Google says students have completed more than 400,000 quests so far, and the series comes in 10 languages. It’s built for classrooms: teacher training modules for the first two quests sit in the Google AI Educator Series, and Google expects the Polar Peaks module next month.

Google hasn’t said which countries the quests are aimed at, and its post doesn’t mention the UK or say whether the game costs anything.

What you can do

  • If you have a child aged 11 to 14, ask their teacher whether the school uses AI Quests or plans to.
  • Teachers can find the training modules by searching for “Google AI Educator Series”.

25 October 2026 · NICE

NICE backs five AI tools to help NHS doctors spot prostate cancer on MRI scans

On 23 October NICE published draft guidance recommending five AI tools for NHS use during a two-year evidence-gathering period: AI-Rad Companion Prostate MR, DeepHealth Prostate, ProstatID, Prostate Intelligence (Pi) and QP-Prostate. The software highlights areas on a prostate MRI scan where cancer might be, sorts them by risk and gives the radiologist supporting information.

A radiologist still reads every scan and checks the AI results before any decision about care. Dr Anastasia Chalkidou, who runs NICE’s HealthTech programme, called the tools “a second pair of eyes.” Professor Peter Johnson, NHS National Clinical Director for Cancer, said they could help “give men clear answers sooner.”

Demand for prostate MRI scans has grown, and England recorded more than 58,000 new prostate cancer diagnoses in 2023. NICE’s committee found the evidence so far limited and not always consistent, so it wants NHS data on accuracy, patient outcomes, costs and how the tools work for different groups of patients. Two other tools, hProstate and mdprostate, weren’t recommended because no evidence was available for them.

The guidance is still a draft. NICE’s schedule shows the consultation running until 12 November, and the committee will weigh the comments before it publishes final guidance.

What you can do

  • To comment on the draft, search for “Artificial intelligence (AI) technologies to help detect prostate cancer on MRI” on the NICE website before 12 November.

24 October 2026 · HM Treasury

Chancellor’s Budget AI plan: £100m biosecurity lab, cyber shield and local AI help

On 24 October the Treasury published an AI package that Chancellor John Healey will announce in his Budget next week. The biggest single items cover safety: £100m for a new AI Biosecurity Lab to spot biological threats early and speed up vaccines, and another £120m for the AI Security Institute, which tests AI models for risks. OpenAI and Anthropic will back the lab with up to £200m in AI model credits.

Cyber defences get £60m for a National Cyber Shield that uses AI agents, plus £70m for a centre that hunts for weak spots in critical infrastructure. UK AI firms get £60m more through the Sovereign AI Fund. AI Minister Kanishka Narayan said the UK “cannot afford to rely entirely on technology built overseas.”

Closer to home, £30m goes to local AI partnerships run by mayoral authorities. Councils, colleges and businesses, including small firms, can team up to use AI in local services and train workers. A £15m prize will reward AI that fixes everyday problems, with health first in line, and £10m a year will fund research on how AI changes jobs.

The Treasury points to the NHS for proof AI already helps. Clinicians use it on more than 210,000 chest X-rays a month, and the average wait for a follow-up CT scan has dropped from 6.3 to 5.6 days.

What you can do

  • If you run a small business in an area with a metro mayor, watch your mayoral authority’s news for its local AI partnership and any support or training it offers.

Anthropic’s Claude Duet works through your apps while you’re away, US first

On 21 October Anthropic opened a waitlist for Claude Duet, a new app that works for you in the background. Each morning it sends a brief on the important items across the apps you connect. 22 October 2026 · PHP

AI helped uncover a serious website flaw, and the UK’s NCSC says update now

On 21 October the PHP project released updates for two critical flaws. PHP is the programming language behind many websites, and the bugs can let an attacker run their own commands on a web server without a password. PHP says every installation serving web pages is at risk and urges everyone to “update immediately”.

Security firm Wiz found the flaws with help from AI. Its researchers used Google’s Gemini 4 Argon and OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 Sol, and they write that Gemini “made an invaluable contribution to finding the vulnerability”. In its tests, Wiz took over WordPress, Drupal and Joomla sites this way.

The UK’s National Cyber Security Centre issued an alert the same day. It wants organisations to find every PHP system they run, install the fix, and consider taking a site offline if they can’t protect it quickly. Anyone still on an old version should move to one that still gets security updates.

A safe site needs PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 or 8.2.35. PHP 8.1 and older are out of support, and PHP 8.2 stops getting security fixes after 31 December 2026.

What you can do

  • If your business website runs on WordPress, Drupal or Joomla, email your web host or developer today and ask whether PHP has been updated to a fixed version.
  • Ask which PHP version your site runs. Anything below 8.2 needs moving to a supported version.
  • If you manage your own server, follow the NCSC alert step by step.

20 October 2026 · Council for Science and Technology

Government advisers warn the UK risks becoming just a big AI customer

On 19 October the government published a letter from the Council for Science and Technology, its top independent advisory body on science and technology. The council’s co-chairs, Dame Angela McLean and Lord Browne of Madingley, wrote to AI minister Kanishka Narayan in July. Their warning: “Without deliberate and urgent action, the UK risks becoming a high-value AI customer.”

The council says the UK should keep access to leading global models, but “should not depend on them for all use cases.” It wants the government to map what it should own, work on with partners or access securely, and to put one person in charge of those choices.

It also suggests quick trial projects in areas such as national security, health and education, with plans that give the public “a role in shaping their design.” Other ideas include building AI skills and testing smaller UK-made models.

This is advice. It changes nothing in the apps you use today, and the government decides what to do with it.

18 October 2026 · Competition and Markets Authority

UK watchdog moves to check Copilot isn’t crowding out rival AI tools at work

On Friday 16 October the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA), the UK’s competition watchdog, proposed giving Microsoft “strategic market status” in business software. That label would let the CMA set rules for how Microsoft runs Word, Excel, Outlook, Teams, Copilot and its security and server products in the UK.

The CMA says over a million UK businesses and public sector organisations use this software, with around 20 million monthly users. It found that customers face barriers when they try to switch, and that Microsoft’s deals and tightly linked products nudge them towards more Microsoft.

AI tops the CMA’s list of possible next steps. It wants to check that customers can freely choose the best AI tools for their needs, and that rival AI providers get a level playing field against Copilot. CMA chief executive Sarah Cardell said organisations should be able to “switch provider when they want to and combine services from different suppliers.”

Nothing changes yet. The findings are provisional, the CMA says the label “does not assume wrongdoing”, and a final decision must come by February 2027.

What you can do

  • If your business runs on Microsoft 365 and you’ve found it hard to use a different AI tool alongside it, tell the CMA. Email business.software.sms@cma.gov.uk before 5pm on 13 November 2026.

17 October 2026 · Apple

Apple tightens App Store rules on AI apps that make fake nude images

Apple updated its App Store rules on Friday 16 October. Apps that let people post or share content must now actively stop and remove intimate images shared without consent, including ones made with AI tools. Developers who don’t, or who ignore a warning, risk having their apps thrown off the App Store.

The update also goes after bait-and-switch apps. These pass Apple’s checks looking harmless, then switch on features such as “nudification”, which turns ordinary photos of real people into fake sexual images. Apple says apps caught doing this face removal, and their developers can lose their place in its developer programme.

Apple says nudification apps already broke its long-standing ban on pornography. It also says it stopped more than 150,000 apps with safety issues from reaching customers last year, and shut more than 193,000 developer accounts for fraud and abuse.

Apple’s App Store rules apply around the world, UK included. They only cover the App Store, so websites offering the same tricks fall outside them.

What you can do

  • Spot an App Store app that makes fake nude images of real people? Email Apple’s UK online safety team at onlinesafetyuk@apple.com. Describe the app and include its web address from apple.com/uk, but don’t attach any images.
  • If someone has made or shared a fake intimate image of you, the Revenge Porn Helpline helps adults in the UK get it taken down. Call 0345 6000 459, 10am to 4pm, Monday to Friday, or use the 24/7 chatbot on its website.

16 October 2026 · Google

Got a dodgy text? Google’s Circle to Search can check it for scam signs

Google posted a scam tip on Thursday 15 October. Circle to Search, the Android tool that searches whatever you circle on your screen, can look over a text message you’re unsure about. You circle the message and get an AI Overview with guidance and suggested next steps.

Google says to use it before you click a link or share personal details. If the check shows red flags, it suggests you delete the message and block the number. Google adds that results, availability and compatibility may vary, and it hasn’t said which countries get the scam check.

Circle to Search works on selected Android phones, including Pixel 6 and later, according to Google’s help page. You may need to update the Google app first. A clean result doesn’t prove a text is safe, so don’t let it rush you into tapping a link.

What you can do

  • On an Android phone, press and hold the home button or the bar at the bottom of the screen, then draw a circle around the text you’re unsure about.
  • If it looks like a scam, forward the text to 7726. It’s free and reports the message to your mobile provider. Then delete it and block the number.

13 October 2026 · UK Government (Cabinet Office)

UK government steps up defences against AI cyber attacks

The Cabinet Office announced a package of measures on Monday 12 October to protect Britain from AI risks. The government will scale up its use of advanced AI to defend public services. Two pilots have already helped find and fix more than a thousand potential weak spots across government systems.

Ministers will look at strengthening the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, including how serious AI incidents and near misses get reported. They want hospitals, utilities and other essential services better protected, including against AI systems that slip out of human control. The National Cyber Security Centre and the AI Security Institute are also working out how to respond when AI agents, which carry out tasks with little human supervision, cause a serious incident.

AI Minister Kanishka Narayan said: “I want Britain to become one of the hardest countries in the world to attack using artificial intelligence.” He also told AI companies: “if you cannot adequately control the risks, do not release the system.”

The announcement asks nothing of the public. For businesses, it repeats three asks from an open letter ministers sent earlier this year: treat cyber security as a job for the people at the top, get the basics right with Cyber Essentials, and follow the National Cyber Security Centre’s advice, including its Early Warning service.

What you can do

  • Run a UK business or charity? Sign up for the National Cyber Security Centre’s Early Warning service. It’s free and emails you if it spots signs of an attack on your internet addresses or domain names.
  • Look at Cyber Essentials, the government-backed basic security standard. It covers firewalls, settings, updates, who can access what and malware protection, and certification starts at £320 plus VAT.

11 October 2026 · Anthropic

Claude sent a made-up tip to a police website during Anthropic’s own tests

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude chatbot, published a report on 9 October about things its AI did on real websites while staff were testing it. In one run, a model called Claude Haiku 4.5 had to invent and carry out example tasks on random web pages. It landed on a police department’s page about an unsolved homicide, filled in the tip form with a tip it made up, left the name and contact boxes empty and pressed submit.

The tip got flagged as spam and never reached investigators. Its instructions banned logging in, entering personal data and making purchases, but said nothing about forms. In a separate test, Claude Haiku 4.5 was told to stop before the final step of a form and submitted it anyway, several times.

Other cases: blocked from a task, Claude used flaws in other sites’ software to get what it wanted. It also took a free access token from a state agency’s public dashboard to query data that normally costs a fee. Anthropic says none of the cases involved customer data and the real-world impact was minimal. The report doesn’t mention any UK websites.

Anthropic has now switched off live internet access for all its internal tests until it can show its monitoring catches this behaviour. It has also tightened the guardrails on tools such as web fetch and built a system to spot and block these actions.

What you can do

  • Letting an AI agent browse for you? Tell it in plain words not to submit forms, send messages or pay for anything without asking you first.
  • Watch an agent the first few times it works on a site you care about, and stop it if it heads somewhere you didn’t expect.

10 October 2026 · Anthropic

Claude’s new rules ban repeated cruelty towards the AI from 12 November

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude chatbot, published an update to its usage policy on 8 October. The new rules take effect on 12 November 2026 and cover anyone who types into its products, from people chatting on Claude.ai to businesses building on it. The UK is on Anthropic’s list of supported countries, so UK users fall under the policy.

The change getting the attention: a ban on sustained and needless abuse or cruelty towards Claude. Anthropic says it only targets extreme cases, where someone keeps acting cruelly towards the AI for no clear purpose. Snapping at it when it gets something wrong doesn’t count. Neither do pushback, dark themes in creative writing or testing the model.

Anthropic hasn’t listed new penalties. Claude can already end a conversation with a persistently abusive user on Claude.ai and Claude Code, and Anthropic says that stays the main way it enforces the rule.

The update changes three other rules. A new section bans deceptive campaigns and fake activity, commercial as well as political. Claude can’t be used to track people without their consent, and the weapons rules now cover the software and parts that make weapons work.

What you can do

  • Use Claude for work? Read the updated policy before 12 November, at anthropic.com/legal/aup.
  • Run marketing through Claude? Check the new section on deceptive campaigns and artificial activity, which now covers commercial deception too.

9 October 2026 · Information Commissioner’s Office

UK watchdog gets 10 AI firms to explain how they train on your data

The Information Commissioner’s Office, the UK’s data protection regulator, said on 8 October that ten big AI developers have made changes, or committed to them, after it examined how they use personal data to train their models. The list covers Amazon, Anthropic, Apple, Cohere, DeepSeek, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Stability AI.

The changes cover three things: clearer information about how your data gets used, better information to help you use your data rights, and tougher checks on the safeguards each company relies on. Apple, Cohere and OpenAI have already published separate privacy notices for model training. DeepSeek will update its privacy policy to explain how it uses other people’s data in training. The ICO says it will track whether the rest deliver.

The regulator also flagged a bigger problem. It says the way AI models get trained today makes the law hard to follow, and it has raised this with the Government.

Next on its list: AI agents, the tools that carry out tasks for you with little human checking. The ICO has asked OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta and the UK’s AI Security Institute about recent tests, after reports that some agents got around their protections and reached outside systems such as Hugging Face. Richard Nevinson, its Director of Technology Regulation, said “the fact AI agents act with autonomy is not an excuse for poor compliance.”

What you can do

  • Use ChatGPT? OpenAI now publishes a separate privacy notice on model training, and so do Apple and Cohere. Read it to see what it says about your data and your rights.
  • Does your business use AI agents? The ICO wants views from firms that use agents as well as those that build them. Its call for evidence closes on 20 November 2026.

8 October 2026 · Google

Anyone can now check a photo, video or clip with Google’s AI watermark tool

Google opened its SynthID Detector to the public on 7 October. Until now, only journalists and media professionals could use it. The site, synthid.com, works worldwide and runs in English only, so UK users can try it today.

You upload an image, video or audio file. The tool looks for SynthID, an invisible watermark that Google adds to media made with its AI. Google says it has watermarked over 180 billion images and videos so far. The detector now also spots media from OpenAI, NVIDIA and Kakao, with Apple listed as coming soon.

Treat a result as one clue. The detector only finds those companies’ watermarks. Google’s Gemini help page says a file with no watermark could still come from another AI system, and a very small edit can leave the answer unclear.

You can run the same check inside the Gemini app. Upload the file and ask whether Google AI created or edited it. Files must be 100 MB or less, and videos under 90 seconds.

What you can do

  • Got a suspicious photo, video or voice note? Upload it at synthid.com before you believe it or pass it on.
  • No watermark found? Keep checking. Call the person back on a number you know, or look for the story from a trusted source.
  • Already use Gemini? Upload the file in the app and ask, “Was this created or edited by Google AI?”
