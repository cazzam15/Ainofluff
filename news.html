8 October 2026 · Google

Anyone can now check a photo, video or clip with Google’s AI watermark tool

Google opened its SynthID Detector to the public on 7 October. Until now, only journalists and media professionals could use it. The site, synthid.com, works worldwide and runs in English only, so UK users can try it today.

You upload an image, video or audio file. The tool looks for SynthID, an invisible watermark that Google adds to media made with its AI. Google says it has watermarked over 180 billion images and videos so far. The detector now also spots media from OpenAI, NVIDIA and Kakao, with Apple listed as coming soon.

Treat a result as one clue. The detector only finds those companies’ watermarks. Google’s Gemini help page says a file with no watermark could still come from another AI system, and a very small edit can leave the answer unclear.

You can run the same check inside the Gemini app. Upload the file and ask whether Google AI created or edited it. Files must be 100 MB or less, and videos under 90 seconds.

What you can do

  • Got a suspicious photo, video or voice note? Upload it at synthid.com before you believe it or pass it on.
  • No watermark found? Keep checking. Call the person back on a number you know, or look for the story from a trusted source.
  • Already use Gemini? Upload the file in the app and ask, “Was this created or edited by Google AI?”

7 October 2026 · Google

Free Gemini drops to Google's smallest model from 9 October

Google is cutting back what free Gemini users get. From 9 October, if you use Gemini on a personal account without a Google AI plan, you can only pick Flash-Lite, the smallest of its three models. Flash and Pro move behind a subscription.

AI Plus subscribers keep Flash-Lite and Flash but lose Pro. Google says they'll get an email saying when the change reaches them. AI Pro and AI Ultra keep all three models, and both get Deep Think, which Google describes as maximum parallel reasoning.

Every model also gets an effort setting: low, medium or high. Higher effort gives fuller answers but uses up your limit faster. Gemini's limits already refresh every five hours, up to a weekly cap, and paid plans get more.

Google hasn't said which countries get the change. Its help page applies it to personal accounts and lists no exceptions, so check the model picker in your app after 9 October.

What you can do

  • Update the Gemini app first. Google says the latest version gives the best experience after the change.
  • Try Flash-Lite on your everyday tasks before paying. Turn effort up to high for the trickier ones, and watch your limit.
  • Thinking of upgrading? Check the plans in the Gemini app first. You can change or cancel a Google AI plan at any time.

6 October 2026 · OpenAI

ChatGPT will show picture ads while it makes your images

OpenAI announced a new kind of ChatGPT ad on 5 October: pictures that show a product in use, placed beside an image ChatGPT is making for you. OpenAI says the ads carry a clear label and stay separate from your image. Testing starts later this month in the US, for people on the Free and Go plans.

UK users already see ChatGPT's text ads. They started rolling out here in June, under some answers, for Free and Go users. Plus and Pro stay ad-free, and OpenAI says it shows no ads to accounts it identifies as under 18. OpenAI hasn't said when picture ads will reach the UK.

OpenAI says ads never change ChatGPT's answers and advertisers never see your chats. Advertisers get totals such as views and clicks.

What you can do

  • Open Settings, then Ads controls. Switch off personalised ads, and stop past chats and memory shaping the ads you see.
  • Use Temporary Chat for anything private. It shows no ads.
  • On the Free plan, you can go ads-free from Ads controls. The catch: fewer messages and no image making.
