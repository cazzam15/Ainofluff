Built with AI

I don't code. I built this whole site with AI anyway.

A website on its own domain, four products for sale and a month of Instagram posts. Here's how it came together, mistakes included.

September 2026 5 min read @ainofluff

In June I had a name, an idea and no website. By the end of one long day I had a live site at ainofluff.co.uk, four digital products on sale and a month of Instagram posts ready to go out.

I've never done a coding course. I typed what I wanted into Claude, copied what it gave me and fixed whatever broke. You're reading the result.

The starting point

I had the name ainofluff and a plan: explain AI to people who haven't tried it yet. I also had a Claude Pro subscription and a free GitHub account. Nothing else. No designer, no developer, no clue what DNS meant.

The build, in order

  1. The website
    I described the site I wanted: dark header, teal accents, two fonts, a grid of posts. Claude wrote the page as one HTML file. I pasted it into GitHub, and GitHub Pages put it online for free. No website builder, no monthly fee.
  2. The domain
    Somebody already owned ainofluff.com, so I bought ainofluff.co.uk from Namecheap. Pointing it at the site meant adding four "A records" and one "CNAME record" in the settings. I had no idea what those were. Claude told me which boxes to fill in and what to type in each one.
  3. The products
    A prompt pack, caption templates, a content planner and a bundle of all three. Claude built the PDFs and spreadsheets, designed the cover images and wrote the product descriptions. I checked each one, asked for changes and uploaded them to Gumroad.
  4. Instagram
    Claude designed the carousel slides and wrote the captions. I picked a profile picture from three options and scheduled a month of posts.

The prompt that did the heavy lifting

A prompt like this one built the homepage:

Copy and change this

I'm building a website for ainofluff, a brand that explains AI in plain English to people who haven't tried it. Make me a single HTML file for the homepage. Use these colours: dark #0F1117, teal #1D9E75, off-white #F5F5F0. Use Space Grotesk for headings and Inter for body text. Include a big headline at the top, a grid of blog posts and an email sign-up box. No frameworks. It needs to work on GitHub Pages.

Every detail in there saves a round of corrections. Leave out the colours and you get Claude's colours. Leave out GitHub Pages and you might get code that won't run there. Swap in your business, your colours and your sections, and you have a starting point for your own site.

Four things that broke

  • The Buy buttons went nowhere

    The first shop page went live with placeholder links. Anyone clicking Buy got nothing. Fix: I swapped in the real Gumroad links and uploaded the page again.

  • The shop disappeared from the menu

    The live homepage had no link to the shop at all. Fix: Claude rebuilt the homepage file with the link in place.

  • Instagram refused to connect

    I'd set the account up through my personal Facebook, and Meta Business Suite wouldn't schedule posts from it. Fix: I made a separate Facebook Page for ainofluff and linked Instagram to that.

  • The spreadsheet looked squashed

    The content planner's rows came out cramped in Google Sheets. Fix: a few rounds of "row 28 is still squashed" until it looked right.

None of these needed coding knowledge. Each one needed me to spot the problem, describe it in plain words and paste in the fix.

The running costs

GitHub hosts the site for nothing. Gumroad charges nothing upfront and takes a cut when something sells. That leaves two bills: the domain once a year and my Claude Pro subscription every month.

Your turn

Pick one small thing you'd build if you knew how. A one-page site for your business. A price list. A spreadsheet that does your sums for you. Describe it to Claude or ChatGPT in as much detail as the prompt above. Then fix what breaks, one problem at a time.
